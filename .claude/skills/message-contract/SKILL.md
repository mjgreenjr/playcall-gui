## Message contract (title / body / urgency)
These rules define the text of every Event, whatever the sink. Default (NFL, cfb):
- Title: "{Away} {away_score} · {displayClock} {period} · {Home} {home_score}" (e.g. "ATL 14 · 0:48 1st · NO 0"; the status section is left out if there is no clock)
- Body: "{start D&D} · {play text} · {end D&D}" (e.g. "1st & 10 · Rush for 11 yards · 2nd & 5"), where D&D is `shortDownDistanceText` from the play's `start` and `end`. The play text is ESPN's, stripped of surrounding whitespace. The start part is left out when there is no `start` (kickoffs); the end part when there is no `end`. The scoring-text baseline still uses the bare play text. ESPN writes a goal-line spot as the team plus 00 ("to the BYU00 TOUCHDOWN"); the body shows it as "BYU end zone", and other spots with a space and no leading zero ("ILL11" as "ILL 11", "BYU01" as "BYU 1"), and a name ESPN cut at six characters is completed from the game's teams ("Maryla22" as "Maryland 22", "OhioSt19" as "Ohio State 19"); display only, the play text is kept as sent. ESPN sends " & Goal" (down 0) on timeouts and some penalties; a D&D that starts with "&" has no down and is left out of the body. The End Period, End of Half and End of Game rows have no usable down and distance either (their `end` differed from the play before's in about 70 of 90 cases, e.g. "2nd & 10" after a gain that left 2nd & 7): their body is the bare text and the header's situation skips them like a timeout. A timeout's own `start` can be stale (after a punt it still shows the fourth down), so a timeout's body, and the GUI header's situation while the last play is a timeout, use the end of the last non-timeout play before it (none after a score or kickoff). The timeout that comes right after a change of possession (a kickoff or punt, a turnover, a turnover on downs) is the official's (each ends with one unless the play scores), and so is the timeout right after a scoring play (the TV timeout before the kickoff: in one Saturday's college feeds all 31 timeouts after a touchdown or field goal were followed by the kickoff, never a snap, though ESPN files each under a team), and so is the timeout at 2:00 of the 2nd or 4th quarter (the two-minute warning), which ESPN logs below 2:00 when the play before it ran through the mark (that play's snap, from its "(02:03)" prefix, was above 2:00), so "Timeout BYU, clock 08:54" there is shown as "Official timeout, clock 08:54"; ESPN files it under a team. That team is the one on offense (36 of 37 college timeouts stamped 2:00 in the 2nd and 4th quarters, checked 2026-10-13 against the weekend's feeds; the exception was HAW @ ASU), and ESPN's `timeoutsUsed` does not charge it (DEL @ MTSU, same day: the 2:00 "Timeout Delaware" left Delaware at 0 used). College timeouts checked against ESPN's counter (`timeoutsUsed` rises when a timeout is charged; 258 timeouts in 39 weekend games, regulation only, 2026-10-10 and 11; the counter is taken as the truth, not checked against a broadcast): the rules above call 23 of 258 wrong (9%). Every timeout play has the same `teamParticipants` flag, charged or not, so it says nothing. ESPN files an official timeout under the team on offense (the 2:00 stoppage: 35 of 35 uncharged were filed under the offense, the one filed under the defense, ASU, was charged), so a timeout filed under the defense is charged (85 of 87 mid-game, 'team' by the rules) and one filed under the offense is ambiguous (65 charged, 12 not: TV timeouts, injuries, reviews are guesses). After a touchdown the rule should depend on the try, which ESPN folds into the touchdown play's text: 15 of 15 before a kick attempt were uncharged (the TV break before the kickoff), 4 of 4 before a two-point try were charged (CAL, M-OH, and both teams at 2:50 in SYR @ UVA). A timeout right after another timeout was charged in 6 of 8 (the rule judges it by the timeout before it, which is not a play). After a score, a turnover or a missed field goal in the last 3:00 of the 4th, 5 of 7 were charged, against 0 of 46 anywhere else. The length of the wait to the next play does not separate charged from uncharged. Injuries: when the play before a timeout says "#n X injured on the play", the first timeout after it filed under the offense was never charged (3 of 3: TEX @ OU Q2 12:12, STAN @ ND Q2 14:07 and Q4 11:58, each the offense's name on an official stoppage while the hurt player was on the other side), and the one filed under the defense was charged (1 of 1, NDSU @ UNLV Q2 7:19, J. Pope hurt: read as a team's strategic timeout, which fits defense-filed timeouts being charged 85 of 87 times; that reading is a guess); four cases, so a small sample. The injured player's number is not a usable signal for whether he stayed in (only tacklers and ball carriers are named in the text). With the rules the data supports (a timeout after a timeout, a two-point try, the 2:00 stoppage filed under the defense) 19 of 258 are still wrong, 14 of them mid-game team timeouts the counter did not charge, so no rule from the plays alone gets the count right; the counter is the source for a college row of circles. Checked live on 2026-10-13 in two full games (DEL @ MTSU, FIU @ JXST, capture from the start): the counter at the break plus the second half's rise gave the right remaining count for every team at every step (3 minus the counter's rise since halftime: FIU 3 at the break and 6 at the end, three timeouts in the last 54 seconds, 0 left; JXST 1 and 4; DEL 1 and 3), and a timeout right after an official one (DEL at 1:46 after the interception stoppage at 1:51) was charged. ESPN's counter is gone once the game ends (blank in the final summary), so only live capture can check it. The two-minute stoppage is not always posted: in 66 college halves the first row after the play carrying the clock past 2:00 was a timeout in 42; whether the play ran through the mark does not decide it (3 of 8 when it did, 39 of 62 when it did not); in 7 of the 24 halves without a row the next snap was stamped exactly 02:00, which looks like a stoppage ESPN did not post. Built for college rows that arrive while the app is running (`collegeTimeoutVerdicts`, `Feed.Charged`): a rise of the counter is a credit (valid 90 seconds, since the counter leads the row by about 10), a new timeout row takes one and is the team's, or finds none and is "Official timeout"; rows seen on the first poll keep the rules above, which are now only the fallback. Live 2026-10-14 (KENN @ MOST): a timeout filed under KENN at 5:57 of the 1st (offense, after an incompletion) was not charged and the broadcast showed no timeout used (a TV break), and one at 14:07 of the 2nd right after a kickoff was charged (counter 0 to 1), against the rules' "official" for any timeout after a kickoff.
- Urgency: normal for scoring plays (`scoringPlay`) or turnovers (`isTurnover`), low otherwise.
  - Kickoffs and punts are low, even if their text contains a keyword ("Kickoff" and "Punt" come from `type.text`). A missed field goal is normal.
  - Plain-text keywords (touchdown, field goal, safety, fumble, interception) are a fallback for plays without the flags above. NFL terms; other sports rely on `scoringPlay`/`isTurnover`.
- Updates: if a scoring play's text changes after it was sent (e.g. the PAT result is added to the touchdown play under the same id), it is re-sent as an update (`Kind: "update"`) with the new text; when the play is stale (`stalePlay`: more than 5 minutes older than the newest play in the feed by `wallclock`, or at least a minute older with the newest play in a later period or half-inning; ESPN rewords old scoring plays and sometimes flips between two wordings, and the real additions, an extra point or an assist, arrived within 5 minutes in a weekend of data) it goes out as a quiet correction (`Revision`), which replaces the row and makes no toast. Updates are sent before new plays. ESPN also posts some plays with partial text (a bare "foul", "enters the game for X") and fills it in later, so basketball fouls and substitutions and NHL penalties and shots (a bare "Missed Shot" later becomes "Joel Farabee saved by Ville Husso") are re-sent the same way when their text changes after they were sent (`revisablePlay`; tracked in memory only, not in state.json). A baseball "pitches to" play is revised too (ESPN rewrites the pitcher after a change), and so is any football play that is not stale, quietly (ESPN corrects yardage, spots and names for a few minutes; hundreds of times a game, so no alerts). Other edits (a shot's description, a removed assist on a non-scoring play) are logged at DEBUG ("Play text changed") but not re-sent. In the GUI router, these corrections (`Event.Revision`) replace the row in the window and never make a toast; a re-sent scoring play does.
- Late football plays: a play from a period whose end an earlier poll already showed (an End Period, End of Half or End of Game play in the feed) is not announced, since nothing from the 1st quarter can follow the end of the 1st (`dropAfterPeriodEnd`; plays that arrive in the same poll as the end are sent). It is marked handled and logged at DEBUG.
- Late plays in the window: a football or baseball play sent under an id below `last_play_id` (ESPN posted it after later plays) carries `Event.Late` and `Event.At` (its `wallclock`); the window's list inserts it below the rows with a later stamp (`Model.ApplyAt`), not on top. A late play without a stamp goes on top, and one never goes above the final. Seen in BUF @ LAR overtime on 2026-10-12: a blocked field goal arrived 90 seconds after three plays that followed it.
- Replaced football plays: ESPN sometimes corrects a play (a kickoff "fair catch" became "Touchback") by dropping its id and posting the corrected play under a new one. A new play with the same period, clock, type and first 30 characters of text as an earlier one whose id has left the feed is that play again, so it is marked handled and not announced (`dropReplaced`; in memory, so a restart forgets it).
- Status behind the plays at kickoff: ESPN posts a period's kickoff play before its status block moves on: 10 to 20 seconds at the start of the game (KENN @ MOST on 2026-10-14: play at 19:33:11, counters at 19:33:32), minutes at the half (the 3rd quarter's kickoff row with the header still on Halftime). The same at the end of the half: ESPN posts the 2nd quarter's End Period row (the NFL's End of Half) before its status says Halftime (GASO @ ODU and ECU @ UAB on 2026-10-15: 41 and 42 seconds, DEL @ MTSU 80; never the other way in those three), so with that row newest and the status still in the 2nd, the game is treated as at halftime and the countdown starts. A football game with plays in the 1st quarter and status "pre", or at halftime or a period break with a newest play (not an End Period row) from a later period, is treated as live in that period (`startedBeforeStatus`, in place on the fetched data, before anything reads it): state "in", the clock from the newest play, and at the start of the game the score from the play's `awayScore`/`homeScore` (a kickoff returned for a touchdown) and no timeouts used.
- Status behind the plays at the end: ESPN posts the End of Game row (college too, text "End of 4th quarter."; the pre-overtime row is `End of Regulation`) before its status goes final: PSU @ MICH on 2026-10-17, row posted 19:11:12, `STATUS_FINAL` 19:11:32, the status in between "in" with no period or clock, and ESPN's records changed around the row. With that row newest and the status not final, the GUI header (`GameStatus.Final`, Detail "Final", no counts or situation) shows Final (`footballGameOver`, a copy of the feed in `publishStatus`); the final event, `Finished` and the end of polling still wait for ESPN, and the GUI's standings refresh waits for State "post".
- College overtime (ECU @ UAB on 2026-10-15, first one seen; no timeout was taken, so ESPN's counter in OT is still unchecked and the circles show nothing): the End of Regulation row was posted at 23:00:42 with the status still in the 4th, the status moved to period 5 at 23:04:55 (the break between regulation and OT; the header sat on "0:00 4th" meanwhile), and the first OT snap was stamped 23:06:38. There is no kickoff in college OT (the first play is a snap from the 25). The OT clock stays at 0:00. After the last play (an interception return touchdown, 23:17:34) the status lost its period and clock about a minute later and went Final at 23:19:37, 2 minutes after the play. OT plays were posted 45 to 60 seconds after their stamps. Not built: an "End of regulation" label for the stretch before the status flips (offered, Mike has not asked for it).
- ESPN does not update a play's `end` for a first down: 10 of the 71 non-scoring plays with "1ST DOWN" in their text had an end that was not 1st (in 6 the next snap started at 1st, so the text was right; the other 4 were two plays overturned on review, whose "1ST DOWN" is the original play's, inside "(Original Play: ...)", and those keep their end) (a 2-yard rush from 3rd & 1 ending "4th & 1", and the ones where a penalty gave the first down, 2026-10-14, KENN @ MOST and WKU @ SHSU). When the text says it, that end is dropped (`playEnd`) from the body, the situation and the timeout's reading of the play before it; the header then has no situation until the next snap.
- A touchdown pass that gained a first down has ", 1ST DOWN" in ESPN's text ("...TOUCHDOWN, clock 09:20, 1ST DOWN", and still there once the extra point is appended); the window and toasts leave it out of scoring plays (`Event.Display`, `touchdownFirstDown`), `Body` and the CLI keep it.
- Football clock quirks (ESPN's data, shown as follows): ESPN writes ", clock 00:00" into a score's text until it knows the time (a poll or two), so the body leaves that out unless the game clock really is 0:00 (the raw text is untouched, so the update with the real time is still detected). ESPN's game clock can also stay at the start of a quarter ("15:00 2nd") for minutes while plays go on (seen in cfb; its scoreboard and summary agreed); when it is more than 45 seconds above the newest play's clock in the same period, that play's clock is shown instead (`footballClock`, NFL and cfb, live games only, not at a period break).
- GUI header layout: one row, mirrored like a broadcast score bug (`headerRow` keeps the middle on the window's center whatever the side widths; in baseball each side's record column is a fixed `roleWidth` and the batter and pitcher names, shortened to an initial and surname, are cut with an ellipsis if still too long). Away: ball (if it has the ball), logo, abbreviation (record under it), score. Home: score, abbreviation (record under it), logo, ball. Football's timeouts sit under each score as circles (drawn, not the ● ○ characters, whose glyphs differ in size and height), filled for each left, centered on it (a gap between score and abbreviation, not a space in the score's text, so the two stay aligned); basketball's timeouts and fouls keep their own row under the header.
- Halftime estimate (NFL and college): `GameStatus.HalftimeEnds` is the first half's last play's `wallclock` (NFL: End of Half; college: the 2nd quarter's End Period) plus the usual break (`nflHalftime` 14:15, from eight games at 13:57 to 15:37 (BUF @ LAR on 2026-10-12: 13:59) and a London one at 19:45; `cfbHalftime` 20:45, median of 45 games with 40 between 19:36 and 21:36, the rest delays; DEL @ MTSU on 2026-10-13 took 21:03, and ESPN's status went STATUS_IN_PROGRESS "End of 2nd Quarter" for about 80 seconds, then STATUS_HALFTIME "Halftime", as assumed); the header shows "Halftime · ~9:40", redrawn each second, and plain "Halftime" once it runs out or when ESPN gives no stamp.
- Rankings (college football and basketball only, `sports.HasRankings`): ESPN's `curatedRank.current` on the scoreboard (99 = unranked) and `rank` in a game summary, kept when 1 to 25 (`espn.Rank`; `Game.AwayRank`/`HomeRank`, `TeamScore.Rank`). The GUI shows the number small, just before the abbreviation, on both teams (picker row: a ranking column sized to the widest, gone when nobody in the list is ranked; header: left of the abbreviation, hidden when unranked); toasts, titles and the CLI do not show it. A ranked game needs about 540 px of header width for the clock to stay on one line, hence the window's default width of 540 (baseball fits in it, with first names shortened to an initial).
- GUI header extras: under the clock, football shows the down and distance and baseball the bases as a diamond of squares on a corner with a home plate outline (`GameStatus.Bases`, "13" is first and third, filled when a runner is on) and the balls, strikes and outs as rows of circles beside it (`GameStatus.Balls/Strikes/Outs`: 3, 2 and 2 circles, no more; a result or inning break shows 0-0), centered together; the text "1-2, 1 out, 2 on" from the newest play that has one stays in titles and the CLI feed, and the window shows no count text; the baseball status line is ESPN's "Top 3rd" (it has no clock). Baseball has no ball marker (neither in the window nor in toasts): the half inning says who bats, and a ball would read as the pitching side. A toast for a pitch adds the batter after the text ("Ball 2 · Sam Antonacci", `Event.Batter`; the CLI output and the window rows do not change). Baseball also shows "P: ..." under the fielding team and "AB: ..." under the batting one (first name as an initial, "P: Y. Yamamoto", to save width), from the newest Start Batter/Pitcher play ("X pitches to Y"; the batter is blank after an at-bat's result until the next batter is announced, and everything is blank after an End Inning). In the postseason (season type 3) the game page shows no season records under the teams, and the picker shows only the series standing, if there is one, as one statement in the row's detail ("Series: LAD 2-1", "Series: Tied 2-2", "Series: LAD WINS" once won, from the scoreboard's `totalCompetitions`; nothing at 0-0), not a record under each team.
- Display text (window and toasts only, `Event.Display`; `Body` and the CLI stay ESPN's text, so the goldens hold): NHL, NBA, WNBA, cbb and MLB play text gets the team after each full player name ("Nikita Zadorov (PHI) hit Noel Acciari (BOS)"), from the box score's rosters (a name two teams share is left alone; football and baseball results use initials or surnames, so they are untouched). An NHL icing, which ESPN gives no team, reads "Icing (BOS)": the puck's shot-from `coordinate` is in the icing team's own end (the next faceoff is there too, 43 of 43), and the team that defends that end is the one whose shots in the period (or the one before, ends switch each period) come from the other end.
- Final: once the game is final and all plays are sent, one more event goes out before polling stops: title "{Away} {away_score} · {shortDetail} · {Home} {home_score}" (e.g. "PIT 24 · Final · CLE 27", "Final/10"), body "Game over", normal urgency (`Kind: "final"`). If `Send` fails, the next poll retries it. Applies to every sport.
- After the final (seen once, KENN @ MOST on 2026-10-14, not acted on): polling stops when the status turns final, but ESPN keeps finishing the game for a few minutes. Its play list went from a kneel-down at 1:07 of the 4th straight to Final (the last drive and the winning field goal were not posted, checked 7 minutes after), and the records under the teams (1-4 each before) changed to 2-4 and 1-5 only after the final, so a header left open on the game keeps the pregame records. Options if it comes up again: poll about 5 minutes past the final (catches both), or add the result to each record ourselves. Neither is built.
- Postseason series: when the game header has a `series` entry with `type: "playoff"`, every title (plays, updates and the final event) ends with the standing in parentheses, leader first: `(LAD 2-1)`, `(Tied 2-2)`, `(BOS WINS)` once a team clinches (best-of from `totalCompetitions`). Nothing is added at 0-0 or outside a playoff series. Wins come from ESPN's `competitors[].wins`, matched to the game's teams by id; ESPN counts completed games only, so the final event adds this game's winner if the series' event list shows it is not counted yet. `Event.Series` carries the text without the parentheses. Its goldens (`mlb`, `wnba` and their replays) were written from the Go build, not the Python. The GUI's game page also tags the game in a playoff series with its place and length (`GameStatus.SeriesInfo`, "NLCS - Game 1 · Best of 7": ESPN's header `gameNote` when it has one, else the game's position in the series' event list), shown even at 0-0, before the standing "Series: LAD 2-0"; titles and the CLI are unchanged.
- MLB, NHL and NBA-rules sports override the above (next sections).
